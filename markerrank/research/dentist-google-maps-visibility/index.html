76% of dentists rank top 3 at their own address. For almost half of them, the top 3 stops within 700 metres.

By the MarkerRank team · Published and last updated 2026-11-24 · Data collected 24 November 2026

We scanned 270 dentist listings in six European cities, one Google Maps grid per practice, centred on its own address. The check most people do, searching from the practice's own door, flatters nearly everyone: 205 of the 270 (76%) were in the top 3 there. Move one grid step away, 500 to 700 metres, and 46% of those 205 were no longer in the top 3 at any of the eight surrounding points. No practice held the top 3 on more than 39% of its grid. The median practice held it on 1 point out of 49.

The charts are identical in all language versions; their labels are in English.

How we did it

Sample270 dentist listings: 45 per city, in Paris, Lyon, Amsterdam, Manchester, Munich and Madrid
How listings were chosenFor each city we searched the generic dentist term plus the city name, in the local language, from a 5 x 5 sweep of points 2.5 km apart around the centre. We kept listings whose Google category looked dental, then drew 45 per city at random (fixed seed) from that pool. Pools held between 125 (Manchester) and 432 (Paris) listings.
ScanOne 7 x 7 grid per listing (49 points), centred on the listing, 500 m between points, so each grid covers about 3 x 3 km. Mobile results, top 20 positions, zoom level 15.
Query"dentiste Paris", "dentiste Lyon", "tandarts Amsterdam", "dentist Manchester", "Zahnarzt München", "dentista Madrid". Every practice in a city got the same query.
Date24 November 2026, one scan per practice
Data sourceGoogle Maps results through a search data provider's API, using the same scan code as the MarkerRank product
Counted as visibleListing present in the first 20 Maps results at that point. "Top 3" and "top 10" are positions within those results.
CostUSD 13.21 in data credits in total, including discovery, an abandoned first run, and robustness checks

All 13,230 grid points returned a result. Nothing in the data names a practice, and none of the per-practice data is published.

Finding 1: the typical practice is in the top 3 at one point out of 49

The median top-3 share was 2%, which is exactly one point. In five of the six cities, that was the median. 20% of practices were in the top 3 at none of the 49 points, including some that were visible lower down. The best single practice reached 19 points (39%).

Manchester is the exception: a median of 18%. Looking at the raw grids, 40 of 45 Manchester practices showed up on the same block of 15 points, which suggests the Manchester results change in blocks rather than gradually. We do not know why, and we did not test it. Treat the Manchester median as real for this query, and not as proof that Manchester dentists are better placed.

Finding 2: visibility falls steeply with distance

Pooling all 270 grids: at the own address, 76% were top 3 and 98% were somewhere in the top 20. At the first ring (500 to 700 m), those figures were 22% and 73%. At 1 km they were 6% and 22%. At the 1.5 km edge they were 1% and 5%.

On average, the first grid point outside the top 20 was 0.76 km from the practice. That is a mean over the 270 grids and it is quantised by the grid spacing, so read it as "about three quarters of a kilometre", not as a precise figure.

Finding 3: "top 3 at my address" says little about the next street

Among the 205 practices that were top 3 at home, 46% were in the top 3 at none of the eight neighbouring points. 62% were absent from the top 20 at all 24 points on the outer ring. The cities differ a lot: Madrid is the sharpest (81% had no top 3 neighbour), Manchester the mildest (8%).

Finding 4: what one scan looks like

Here is one real scan, anonymised: no name, no street, no map. We picked it as a clear illustration, not as a typical case (it is a practice that is top 3 at its address and absent from the whole outer ring). It is 1st at its own address, between 1st and 17th within 700 m, between 3rd and 11th at 1 km, and absent from the top 20 at every point 1.5 km out.

What this means for agencies

If you report local rankings to clients, this is the "what am I paying for" problem in numbers. A client who searches from the practice's own chair sees a top 3 listing, and a single-point rank report agrees. The same listing can be invisible a few hundred metres away, where the patient actually is. A report that shows only the address-level rank can read as success while the grid underneath is mostly empty.

A grid does not tell you why, and it does not tell you what to change. It does show what a client is really getting at each distance, and it is a cheaper conversation to have with a map in front of you than without one. Limits (please read)

  • Sampling bias. Practices were found through Google Maps results for the query, so we only studied listings Google already shows somewhere in a city-wide sweep. Practices that never appear for that query are not in the sample. The pools (125 to 432 per city) are not counts of all dentists. Our dental category filter is a text match and may have let in or kept out a few listings.
  • One query per city. A generic "dentist plus city" query is not the query every practice cares about. Practices that rank for neighbourhood terms, treatments or their own name are not captured.
  • One scan, one day. A single scan is a snapshot. Rank positions vary by time of day, device, personalisation and Google's own tests. We did not repeat scans, so we cannot report how stable they are.
  • Grid size. The grid covers about 3 x 3 km. We say nothing about visibility beyond 1.5 km from a practice, and "across their whole city" would be an overreach. We did not use a wider grid for the main data: a first run at 2 km spacing (65 practices before we stopped it) mostly returned 1 point in 49, so we switched to 500 m and discarded the coarse data, which we kept privately.
  • Robustness check. On a subset of 8 practices per city (48), the median stayed at 1 point of 49 with a wider map view (zoom 13) and with 1 km spacing. With zoom 13 one practice held the top 3 on 71% of its grid, so "no practice above 39%" holds for the main settings and not for every setting.
  • Sample size. 45 per city is enough to see a pattern, too small for narrow city rankings. We do not rank cities against each other.
  • No causes. We measured where listings appear, not why. Nothing here shows that moving, reviews, categories or any other change would alter a result.
