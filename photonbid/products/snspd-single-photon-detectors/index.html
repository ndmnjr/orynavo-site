About this product category

A superconducting nanowire single photon detector (SNSPD) registers single photons with high efficiency and fine timing. It works at cryogenic temperature, so a purchase usually includes a closed cycle cooler or cryostat, readout electronics and installation. Other single photon detectors, such as SPAD modules and photon counting modules, cover shorter or cheaper needs.

Buyers are mostly university physics and quantum optics groups, research institutes and national quantum programmes. Many purchases are small or have one possible supplier, so they are often direct awards or negotiated awards. That is why the award history below matters as much as the open notices.

Not every purchase is public: direct awards and small buys do not appear on TED. Always check the original notice before deciding to bid.

Questions

Are SNSPD systems bought by public tender?

Sometimes. Above the EU threshold a tender or a published award is required, and many purchases are negotiated without a call because only one supplier can deliver. Below the threshold, national rules apply and direct awards are common.

Where do these SNSPD and single photon detectors notices come from?

Open notices come from TED (ted.europa.eu) and, where shown, national portals. Each row links to the original notice, which is the authoritative source.

What does winner of record mean?

It is the supplier named in the published award notice. A reseller can be the winner of record while supplying another maker's product.

Is this a price list or a forecast?

No. Values are as published in award notices, in the original currency, and 'value not published' means the notice shows none or a placeholder. Past awards do not predict future purchases.
