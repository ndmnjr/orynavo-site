SNSPD tenders: how public labs in Europe buy single photon detectors

Most SNSPD systems bought by European public labs go to universities and research institutes, and the price decides the route. Below the EU threshold of EUR 140,000 or EUR 216,000, many never reach a public tender. Above it, they appear on TED. The competitor winner map for SNSPD contracts shows who won the 34 core awards in our data, up to 22 November 2026.

This guide follows one purchase from the lab to the award notice, with real records.

Who buys SNSPD systems in Europe

Three kinds of public buyer show up again and again in published awards:

  • University chairs and institutes. The University of Stuttgart bought an SNSPD for 1550 nm for EUR 270,000 (TED 565889-2026). The University of Jena paid EUR 163,000 for an SNSPD system (TED 511766-2026).
  • Research organisations. Max Planck, Fraunhofer, DLR, CNR and the Austrian Institute of Technology all appear as buyers in our data. AIT bought an SNSPD system for EUR 188,000 (TED 700857-2024).
  • National quantum programmes and central buyers. GRNET bought 16 SNSPD detectors for the Greek HellasQCI network for EUR 356,990 (TED 325195-2024). In the UK, UK Shared Business Services bought a single photon detector module (Contracts Finder).

Repeat buyers matter more than one-off ones. The University of Stuttgart placed 4 SNSPD awards in our data, all with the same supplier. The German SNSPD buyer list shows the pattern country by country.

Which procedure applies to an SNSPD purchase?

The estimated value decides it. Above the EU threshold the buyer must run an EU procedure and publish on TED; below it, national rules apply, and in some countries that means a direct award with no call for competition.

For 2026 and 2027 the EU supply thresholds are EUR 140,000 for central government bodies and EUR 216,000 for sub-central authorities, set by Delegated Regulation (EU) 2025/2152. A single-channel or small multi-channel SNSPD often sits close to those lines, which is why the same product can be tendered in one country and directly awarded in another.

Three routes appear in the award records:

  1. Open procedure. The lab publishes a contract notice, suppliers bid, the award notice names the winner. DTU bought two 32-channel SNSPD units this way for EUR 1,068,500 (TED 406863-2025).
  2. Negotiated procedure without prior publication. The buyer argues that only one supplier can deliver and publishes the award afterwards. The University of Copenhagen bought an SNSPD for EUR 270,000 this way (TED 513499-2025), and the Max Planck Institute of Quantum Optics did the same, with no value published (TED 29562-2026).
  3. National direct award. In Italy, supplies below EUR 140,000 can be awarded directly under D.Lgs. 36/2023, art. 50. INFN Roma 2 bought an SNSPD service for EUR 10,494 that way (ANAC). Our guide to direct awards in Italy covers this route in detail.

Route 2 hurts most if you are not on the buyer's radar. By the time the notice appears, the order is placed.

What public buyers paid

Published values give you a price band to position against. Across 9 awards for single SNSPD systems in our data, values ran from EUR 75,000 to EUR 270,000, with a median of about EUR 184,000. Large multi-channel systems sit far higher, up to EUR 1,068,500 at DTU. Services and repairs land around EUR 10,000 to EUR 12,000, based on two Italian awards.

Where the notices appear

Above-threshold notices from EU buyers go to TED. UK buyers moved to Find a Tender on 1 January 2021, and lower-value UK contracts appear on Contracts Finder. Below-threshold purchases sit on national platforms such as ANAC's legal publicity platform in Italy or e-Zamówienia in Poland. Our guide on finding photonics tenders on TED and national portals lists the search settings for each.

How to be known before the tender opens

A tender is the last step of a purchase. The lab chose the technology months earlier, often after talking to two or three suppliers. A few habits put you in that conversation:

  • Track who bought before. A lab that bought a system years ago may need an upgrade, more channels or service.
  • Watch contract end dates. Some UK award notices publish a contract period, which gives you a date to call.
  • Follow new research groups. A new chair or a funded quantum project usually comes with an equipment budget.
  • Read the competitor's wins. If one supplier won every award at a buyer, your pitch is a second source, a different wavelength or channel count, or a better service offer.

FAQ

Do universities have to tender every SNSPD purchase?

No. EU rules require a tender above the threshold, and buyers can still use a negotiated procedure without publication when only one supplier can deliver. Below the threshold, national rules apply, and Italy allows direct awards for supplies under EUR 140,000.

How much does an SNSPD system cost a public lab?

In the published awards we hold, single SNSPD systems cost EUR 75,000 to EUR 270,000. Large multi-channel systems reached EUR 1,068,500. These are small samples and published values only.

Where can I see who won an SNSPD contract?

The award notice names the winner. Search TED, Find a Tender or Contracts Finder for the buyer and product, or see our map of SNSPD contract winners.
