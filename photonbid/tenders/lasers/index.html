What buyers look for in lasers tenders

Laser tenders range from research systems (ultrafast, tunable, high-power) to laser sources inside larger instruments such as microscopes or processing machines. CPV 31712 and 38636 are the codes most often used for laser equipment, but integrated systems are often classified under the host instrument.

Typical requirements are wavelength, output power or pulse energy, pulse duration, beam quality, stability, safety class and interlocks, cooling, and service/installation by the supplier. Many notices permit equivalent products.

Public buyers above the EU thresholds must publish on TED, in the buyer's language, and each notice names the CPV code(s), deadline, lots and where the tender documents can be downloaded. Below the thresholds, notices may appear only on national portals. Not every purchase is public: direct awards and small buys do not appear here.

Questions about lasers tenders

Which CPV codes are used for laser tenders?

Common ones are 38636100 (lasers, other than laser diodes) and the 31712 family, but laser components often sit in laboratory or measurement codes. Check the full notice text and lot descriptions, not only the CPV.

How do I bid on lasers tenders?

At a high level: find the notice, read the tender documents and the selection and award criteria, register on the buyer's e-procurement portal if required, ask clarification questions before the deadline, and submit a compliant offer. The original notice states the exact procedure and deadline.

What is a CPV code?

CPV (Common Procurement Vocabulary) is the EU's classification for what is being bought. Each notice carries one or more CPV codes, which you can use to search TED, although the same product can sit under different codes.
